Gold

A quarter off the record; the Fed is still hiking

Gold settled at $4,000 on 11 November, 25% below its 29 January record close of $5,318. The Federal Reserve raised rates in September, for the first time since 2023, to 3.75-4.00%, two members voted for more in October, and October CPI rose 0.4%. The 10-year real yield is 2.78%, 89bp above its level at the record, and the dollar index is up 7%. Our model, fitted to weekly changes over 2006-23, takes 3.8% off gold for each 100bp on real yields and 1.0% for each 1% on the dollar. Together they explain 9 points of the 25-point fall.

The rest is a premium deflating. From end-2023 to the record gold rose 157%; rates, the dollar and gold’s 8% a year drift account for 24%. A third of the excess has gone, yet gold still trades 62% above the model path of $2,470 and 9% above the 1980 peak in today’s dollars. Holders are paying for the official bid: central banks bought 675t in nine months, 6% more than a year earlier. Two cautions. Only 120t of the third quarter’s 321t was reported, and the first quarter was revised from 244t to 57t. And price-sensitive buyers have stepped back: jewellery fell 17% and bars and coins 19% in the quarter.

We rate gold Neutral with a Dec-27 target of $4,400, 10% above the price (ladder: −5% to +15%), the weighted value of three cases. Base (50%): the Fed raises twice more, real yields ease to 2.4% and the premium holds, $4,530. Bull (25%): the Fed reverses, real yields return to 1.9% and the premium rebuilds, $5,310. Bear (25%): real yields reach 3.25% and half the premium goes, $3,260. Light futures positioning is no reason to buy: the nine times since 2009 it reached the bottom fifth of its three-year range, gold returned 4% over the next year against 11% for all weeks. In rupees the fall is 21%, cushioned by May’s duty rise to 15%; reversing it would cost holders 8%.
